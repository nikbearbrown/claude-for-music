# East of the Sun and West of the Moon

SONG:

Once on a time there was a poor husbandman who had so many children
That he had not much of either food or clothing to give them
Pretty children they all were
But the prettiest was the youngest daughter
Who was so lovely there was no end to her loveliness

One Thursday evening late in the fall of the year
The weather was wild and rough outside
It was cruelly dark
Rain fell and wind blew
Till the walls of the cottage shook again

Then something gave three taps on the window pane
The father went out to see what was the matter
And outside stood a great big White Bear
Good evening to you said the White Bear
Will you give me your youngest daughter
If you will I will make you as rich as you are now poor

At first the lassie said no outright
But in time she thought better of it
She washed and mended her rags
Made herself as smart as she could
And when the White Bear came again
She climbed upon his back and rode away

Are you afraid said the White Bear
No she was not
Mind and hold tight by my shaggy coat
And then there is nothing to fear
So they rode a long long way
Till they came to a great steep hill

A door opened in the hill
And inside was a castle
With many rooms all lit up
Rooms gleaming with silver and gold
There was a table ready laid
And it was all as grand as grand could be

At night when she had gone to bed
A man came and lay beside her
He was the White Bear
Who threw off his beast shape in the dark
But she never saw him
For he always came after the light was out
And was gone before the dawn

So things went on happily for a while
But at last she began to grow silent and sorrowful
For she went about all day alone
And longed to go home
To see her father and mother
And brothers and sisters

The White Bear said perhaps there was a cure
But she must promise one thing
Not to talk alone with her mother
Only when the rest were by to hear
Else she would bring bad luck on both of them

One Sunday the White Bear carried her home
And there was such joy
There was no end to it
Her family had everything they wished
And all wanted to know how she got on
Where she lived

She tried not to go alone with her mother
But somehow her mother got round her at last
And the lassie told the whole story
How every night a man came to her
As soon as she had put out the light
And how she had never seen him

Her mother said it might well be a Troll
And gave her a bit of candle
Light that while he is asleep she said
But take care not to drop the tallow on him

So that night the White Bear fetched her again
And once more the man came to lie beside her
At dead of night
She rose and lit the candle
And the light fell on the loveliest Prince
One ever set eyes on

She loved him so deeply at once
That she thought she could not live
If she did not kiss him then and there
So she kissed him
But three hot drops of tallow fell on his shirt
And he woke

What have you done he cried
Now you have made us both unlucky
Had you only held out this one year
I should have been freed
For my stepmother has bewitched me
So that I am a White Bear by day
And a man by night

Now all ties are snapped between us
Now I must go from you to her
She lives in a castle
That stands east of the sun and west of the moon
And there too is a Princess with a nose three ells long
And she is the wife I must have now

She wept and took it ill
But there was no help for it
Go he must
Then she asked if she might go with him
No she might not
Then tell me the way she said
And I will search you out

Next morning when she woke
Both Prince and castle were gone
She lay on a little green patch
In the midst of a gloomy thick wood
And by her side lay the same bundle of rags
She had brought from her old home

So she set out on her way
And walked many many days
Till she came to a lofty crag
Under it sat an old hag
Playing with a gold apple
The hag did not know the way
But lent her a horse
And gave her the gold apple

Then she came to another old hag
Who gave her a gold carding comb
Then to a third
Who gave her a golden spinning wheel
And told her to ride on
To ask the East Wind

The East Wind had heard of the castle
But had never blown so far
So he carried her to the West Wind
The West Wind carried her to the South Wind
And the South Wind carried her
To the North Wind
Who was oldest and strongest of them all

The North Wind knew the castle well
He puffed himself up and blew her away
High through the air
Over storm and sea
Till at last he cast her ashore
Under the windows of the castle
That lay east of the sun and west of the moon

Next morning the lassie sat under the window
And played with the gold apple
The Princess with the long nose saw it
And wanted it
The lassie said she would give it
Only if she might go to the Prince
And be with him that night

Yes she might
But when she came up to the Prince
He was fast asleep
She called him and shook him
And wept sore
But she could not wake him

The next day she sat below the window
And carded with the gold comb
Again the Princess wanted it
Again the lassie traded it
For one night with the Prince
But again he was fast asleep
And she could not wake him

The third day she sat outside
And spun with the golden spinning wheel
The Princess wanted that too
And once more the lassie asked
Only for one night with the Prince
This time some Christian folk nearby
Had heard her weeping and praying
On the nights before
And they told it to the Prince

So when the Princess came with her sleepy drink
The Prince only pretended to drink it
And threw it over his shoulder
That night when the lassie came in
She found the Prince wide awake
And told him the whole story
Of how she had come to find him

You have come in the very nick of time
Said the Prince
Tomorrow is to be our wedding day
But now I will not have the Long Nose
You are the only woman in the world
Who can set me free

The next day the Prince said
He wished to see what his bride was fit for
He had a fine shirt for his wedding shirt
But it had three spots of tallow on it
And he would have no bride
But the woman who could wash them out

The Princess with the long nose
Scrubbed and rubbed
But the more she washed
The bigger the spots grew
Then her mother tried
Then all the Trolls tried
And the shirt grew blacker and uglier still

Then the Prince called for the beggar lassie outside
She came in
Took the shirt
Dipped it in the water
And almost at once it was white as driven snow
And whiter still

Yes you are the lassie for me
Said the Prince
At that the old hag flew into such a rage
That she burst on the spot
And the Princess with the long nose after her
And the whole pack of Trolls after her
And no one ever heard a word of them again

Then the Prince and Princess
Set free all the poor Christian folk
Who had been carried off and shut up there
And they took with them
All the silver and gold
And fled far away
From the castle that lay east of the sun
And west of the moon

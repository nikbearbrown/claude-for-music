# Crown Him with Many Crowns


**Date/source note:** 1851

## Background

1851 He on his head were many crowns. Revelation 19. The original form of this hymn was
written by Matthew, Bridges, and consisted of six eight-line stanzas. He thought of his
hymn as a sermon and song, based on Revelation 19. Verse 12, and on his head were many
crowns. He called his hymn the song of the seraphs. Matthew, who once wrote a book in
dimming Roman Catholics, ended up converting to Catholicism in 1848. He followed John
Henry Newman out of the Church of England. In 1874, God-free-thring, a staunch Anglican
clergyman, feared that some of Bridges v.

smacked too much of Catholic doctrine. Verse 2, for example, said, Crown him the virgin
son, the God incarnate born, whose arm those crimson trophies one, which now his brow
adorn. Fruit of the mystic rose, as of that rose the stem. The root went smersy ever
flows, the babe of Bethlehem. It seems odd to us now that such a verse would cause
controversy, but in the end God-free-rode six new verses for the same song

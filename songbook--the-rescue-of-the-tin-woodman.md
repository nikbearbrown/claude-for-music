# The Rescue of the Tin Woodman

SONG:

The Rescue of the Tin Woodman

they found him—rusted, axe raised (high
a man (of tin) with a sigh,
frozen in shadows, alone,
dorothy (and scarecrow) standing close,
set him free (a yearning sigh)
axe dropped low, he joins their stride.

she runs quick with the oil-can,
frees his neck, his hands, his feet,
and each limb, bound by rust,
now moving—his heart still incomplete.

morning light (she woke to find)
Toto chasing birds, air kind,
they heard that groan, through trees and stone,
there stood a figure, locked in time.

he speaks of a love, of warmth he knew,
of a Munchkin girl lost from view,
the witch’s curse, his heart did steal,
and left him with only tin to feel.

he says, “i’ve no heart, but still i seek
that love which beats—when all feels bleak.”

they walk, three shadows stretching long,
forest thickens, echoes strong,
his axe swings wide (silver, gold)
clearing a path, a tale retold.

he spoke of love that slipped away,
a curse by the witch, that fateful day,
each limb replaced by the tinsmith’s hand,
the feeling lost, replaced by sand.

“i need a heart,” he says, without a pulse,
for the warmth i miss, in the quiet of dusk.

scarecrow says, “my head’s dry, no brains inside,
yet i’ll seek Oz—my path to guide.”
tin man sighs, “it’s love i seek,”
for life without heart leaves the spirit weak.

they walk where trees grow thick, shadows play,
each step clearing a new way,
for love is all, and all is dust,
no heart, yet he must seek where love may rest.

and down the road, the journey takes,
till green fields call and hearts awake,
for brains, or hearts, or the dreams they sow,
they walk together where shadows grow.

and with his axe, he clears the way,
forest bends, yet does not sway,
three souls bound in silent trust,
walking through the trees, to where love finds its dust.

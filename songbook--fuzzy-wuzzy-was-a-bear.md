# Fuzzy Wuzzy was a bear,

Fuzzy Wuzzy was a bear,
Fuzzy Wuzzy had a name that could mislead or snare.
For though they called him Fuzzy, you see,
He was as bald as a bear could be.

Fuzzy Wuzzy, not so fuzzy,
What’s in a name that makes it so buzzy?
A bear with no hair, still called Fuzzy Wuzzy,
Perhaps it’s a lesson in irony, quite cushy.

No fur on his head, no fur on his toes,
No fur from his ears, no fur on his nose.
He’d look in the mirror, give a sigh and a shrug,
For a bear named Fuzzy, he felt quite snug.

Fuzzy Wuzzy, not so fuzzy,
What’s in a name that makes it so buzzy?
A bear with no hair, still called Fuzzy Wuzzy,
Perhaps it’s a lesson in irony, quite cushy.

Fuzzy Wuzzy went to town,
Bought a hat that was big and brown.
"How can a hat fit a fuzzy-less bear?"
But on his bare head, it had flair to spare.

Buzzy bears buzzed by with beary big bites,
Bold bears bounded over bright beams of light.
But Fuzzy stayed still, with his bald little head,
And smiled with delight, though his fuzziness fled.

Fuzzy Wuzzy, not so fuzzy,
What’s in a name that makes it so buzzy?
A bear with no hair, still called Fuzzy Wuzzy,
Perhaps it’s a lesson in irony, quite cushy.

But Fuzzy he was, to friends far and near,
In their hearts and their laughs, he brought much cheer.
For what’s in a name but what we declare?
Fuzzy Wuzzy’s essence was rare.

Fuzzy Wuzzy found a fuzzy peach pie,
While buzzy bears buzzed and baked bees flew by.
But Fuzzy stayed cool, without fuzz on his fur,
For being bald never caused him to stir.

Fuzzy Wuzzy, not so fuzzy,
What’s in a name that makes it so buzzy?
A bear with no hair, still called Fuzzy Wuzzy,
Perhaps it’s a lesson in irony, quite cushy.

So next time you hear someone called Fuzzy Wuzzy,
Remember our bear, and don’t be too hasty.
For names are just words, and it’s not very fair,
To judge a book, or a bear, by the hair that’s not there.

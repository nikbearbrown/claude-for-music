# A Visit from St. Nicholas” (1823) by Clement Clarke Moore

SONG:


“A Visit from St. Nicholas” (1823) by Clement Clarke Moore
Also known as “’Twas the Night Before Christmas,” this is one of the most iconic Christmas poems in English literature.

“Ring Out, Wild Bells” (1850) by Alfred, Lord Tennyson
A lyrical call for renewal and peace during the holiday season.

“Christmas Bells” (1863) by Henry Wadsworth Longfellow
Inspired by the Civil War, this poem reflects on despair and hope at Christmas.

“Minstrels” (1917) by William Wordsworth
Celebrates the Christmas story and its connection to music and joy.

“The Oxen” (1915) by Thomas Hardy
A nostalgic and reflective poem about Christmas Eve traditions and faith.

“The House of Christmas” (1912) by G.K. Chesterton
A meditation on the incarnation of Christ and the concept of home.

“The Burning Babe” (1595) by Robert Southwell
A religious allegory of the Nativity and Christ’s sacrifice.

“A Christmas Carol” (1843) by Christina Rossetti
Not to be confused with Dickens' story, this poem captures the essence of humility and joy in Christ’s birth.

“In the Bleak Midwinter” (1872) by Christina Rossetti
Later set to music as a beloved Christmas carol, it reflects on the Nativity's simple beauty.

“The Three Kings” (1860) by Henry Wadsworth Longfellow
Tells the story of the Magi’s journey to Bethlehem.

“A Hymn on the Nativity of My Savior” (1629) by Ben Jonson
A deeply spiritual reflection on the birth of Christ.

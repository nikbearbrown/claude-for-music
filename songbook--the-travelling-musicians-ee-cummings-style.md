# the travelling musicians - ee cummings style

SONG:

the travelling musicians - ee cummings style

once there was a donkey
(old as the dust and creaky with rust) who thought, “no more fields
no more load—no more farmer’s grumbling, goodbye, adios! i’ll
become a musician”

down the road he met a dog, all pant and droop—
“come along,” brayed donkey, “we’ll sing for our supper!”

then, paws soft as whispers, a cat joined (nearly drowned,
mind you) with a song stuck in her throat,
“why not?” she sighed, “i’ll go too”

last came a rooster (crowing his fate to the sky)
“soup pot tomorrow!” he cried with a cluck,
“then come,” said donkey, “we’ll make a fine noise”

and so four friends marched towards the city— till night fell thick and dark, a shadow quilt
draped upon their wandering heads—

till a light sparked in the woods (a house—
a feast—a raucous crowd of robbers within)

they formed a grand parade (donkey tall, dog next,
cat balanced and rooster perched)
then a cue! (oh, what a tune—a bray, a bark,
a hiss, a crow, all smashed through glass!)

robbers fled, in a fright of claws and caws
(ghosts, they’d swear)

now safe in the warmth of straw and hearth,
they laughed to sleep and kept the house as home,

if you listen closely,
you may hear their tune—of bray and bark
and cluck, and a hiss in the moon

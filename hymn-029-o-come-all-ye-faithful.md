# O Come, All Ye Faithful


**Date/source note:** 1743

## Background

Matthew chapter 2 verse 11 John Francis weighed author of this hymn pounded out of
England in 1745. He was a Roman Catholic layman and Lancashire, but because of
persecution arising from the Jacobite rebellion, streams of Catholics fled to France and
Portugal, where communities of English-speaking Catholics appeared. But how could he, a
refugee, support himself? In those days, the printing of musical scores was cumbersome
and copying them by hand was an art. In the famous Roman Catholic College and Ministry
Center in Duet, France, Wade taught music and became renowned as a copyist of musical
scores.

His work was exquisite. In 1743, Wade 32 had produced a copy of a Latin Christmas Carol
beginning with the phrase, a des dé fédéless, l'étée triumphantesse. At one time
historians believed he had simply discovered an ancient hymn by an unknown author, but
most scholars now believe Wade himself composed the lyrics. Seven original hand-copied
manuscripts of this Latin hymn have been found, all of them bearing Wade's signature.
John Wade passed away on August 161786 at age 75. His obituary honored him for his
beautiful manuscripts that adorned chapels and homes.

As time passed, English Catholics began returning to Britain and they carried Wade's
Christmas Carol with them. More time passed, and one day an Anglican minister named
Reverend Frederick Oakley, who preached at Margaret Street Chapel in London, came across
Wade's Latin Christmas Carol. Being deeply moved, he translated it into English for
Margaret Street Chapel. The first line of Oakley's translation said, ye faithful,
approach ye. Somehow ye faithful approach ye didn't catch on, and several years later
Oakley tried again.

By this time Oakley II was a Roman Catholic priest having converted to Catholicism in
1845. Perhaps his grasp of Latin had improved because as he repeated over and over the
Latin phrase, a des dé fédéless, l'étée triumphantesse. He finally came up with a
simpler, more vigorous, oak-um, all ye faithful, joyful and triumphant. So two brave
Englishmen, Catholics, lovers of Christmas and lovers of hymns, living a hundred years
apart, writing in two different nations, combined their talents to bid us come, joyful
and triumphant, and adorim born the King of Angels.

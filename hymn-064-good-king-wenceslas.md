## Background

an Anglican minister who lived nearly a thousand years apart. Wencesloss was born in
Bohemia in modern Czechoslovakia in the early 900s. His father, the Czech ruler, Duke
Ratoslav, gave him a good education supervised by his godly grandmother. When his father
died, Wencesloss, seeing his mother mishandle affairs of state, stepped in at age 18,
seizing the reigns of government.

From the beginning he proved a different sort of king. He sought good relations with
surrounding nations, particularly with Germany. He took steps to reform the judicial
system, reducing the number of death sentences and the arbitrary power of judges. He
encouraged the building of churches and showed heartfelt concern for the poor. He
reportedly cut firewood for orphans and widows, often carrying the provisions on his own
shoulders through the snow. Wencesloss' brief reign ended suddenly. His pagan and
rebellious brother, Bohluslav, murdered him on September 28, 929, as he left for church.

His people venerated him as a martyr, and today Wencesloss is the patron saint of
Czechoslovakia. He would be hardly remembered however, but for John Mason Neal, an
Anglican minister with a passion for returning church architecture and music to their
ancient grandeur. Neal helped establish a committee to investigate and restore
dilapidated church buildings in Great Britain. He was particularly upset at the ugly
stoves installed the heed churches in Victorian times. The slacking the hymns of Isaac
Watts, he also sought to return church music to its medieval roots.

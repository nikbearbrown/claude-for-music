# Jesus Loves the Little Children


**Date/source note:** before 1895

## Background

1895. Let the little children come to me and do not forbid them for as such is the
kingdom of God. Luke chapter 18, verse 16. Almost everyone knows Jesus loves the little
children, but few of us have sung the three verses that go along with that chorus. More
to many people realize this was originally a civil war ballad. George Frederick Rude was
born into a large family in Sheffield, Massachusetts in 1820, and showed signs of
musical genius. By age 13, he boasted that he could play 13 different instruments. As a
young adult, he taught music in Boston and New York, and he also composed music and
served as church organist.

In 1855, he offered a song called Rosalie the Prairie Flower to his publisher for the
hefty sum of $100. Rude's publisher, not thinking it worth that much, offered Rude a
royalty plan instead. In time, Rude grossed thousands of dollars from Rosalie, which
helped establish him financially. The outbreak of the civil war deeply affected George,
and he immediately began using his gifts to advance the Union War effort, writing a host
of patriotic songs to rally the morale of the Noroth. As a serious classical composer,
he was embarrassed at the simple, martial music coming from his pen, so he signed them
with a name, Wurzel, the German word for Rude.

Among his most popular pieces was a ballad entitled, Trap, Tramp, Tramp. In the prison
cell I set, thinking Mother Deer of you, and our bright and happy home so far away, and
the tears they fill my eyes, spite of all that I can do, though I try to cheer my
comrades and be gay. Trap, tramp, tramp, the boys are marching. Cheer up comrades, they
will come, and beneath a starry flag we shall breathe the air again of the free land in
our own beloved home. After the civil war, the melody remained popular, but the words
were dated.

A minister named Claire Herbert Wulston, a lyricist whom Rude occasionally used, wrote
new verses and a chorus. And that's how a civil war ballad about a soldier in prison
became one of the most popular children's choruses in history

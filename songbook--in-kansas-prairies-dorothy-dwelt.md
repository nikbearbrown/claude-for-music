# In Kansas prairies, Dorothy dwelt,

SONG:


In Kansas prairies, Dorothy dwelt,
With Uncle Henry, and Aunt Em as well.
Their house was small, their lives quite plain,
In the midst of the great gray plain.

No garret high, no cellar deep,
Just a cyclone cellar for them to keep,
In case the winds began to roar,
And cyclones swept across the floor.

No laughter rang, no joy was found,
In Aunt Em’s eyes, the sparkle drowned.
Uncle Henry toiled from dawn till night,
No time for joy, no time for light.

But Toto, a little black dog so spry,
Brought laughter to Dorothy’s eye.
They played all day, in sun or rain,
And Toto saved her from growing gray.

But one day came, the sky turned dark,
Uncle Henry feared the coming mark.
A cyclone approached, its howl did wail,
Dorothy’s heart began to quail.

Aunt Em cried out, “Run, Dorothy, run!
To the cellar, child, quickly, come!”

**Date/source note:** 1855

## Background

1855 he received word that his mother was facing a crisis. Joseph wrote this poem and
sent it to her. Mrs. Scriven evidently gave a copy to a friend who had it published
anonymously, and it quickly became a popular hymn, though no one knew who had written
it. Meanwhile Joseph fell in love again. But tragedy struck a second time when his
fiance, Eliza Catherine Roche, contracted tuberculosis and died in 1860 before their
wedding could take place. To escape his sorrow, Joseph poured himself into ministry,
doing charity work for the Plymouth brethren and preaching among the Baptists.

He lived a simple, obscure life in Port Hope, Canada, cutting firewood for widows and
giving away his clothes and money to those in need. He was described as a man of short

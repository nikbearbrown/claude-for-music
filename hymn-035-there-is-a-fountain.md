# There Is a Fountain


**Date/source note:** 1772

## Background

1772. Whom God set forth as a propitiation by His blood through faith to demonstrate His
righteousness because in His forbearance God has passed over the sins that were
previously committed. Romans 3.25 William Cooper is one of God's gracious gifts to those
suffering from depression. Like the psalmist who cried, why are you cast down on my
soul? Psalm 42 verse 5. Cooper shows us that our emotional struggles often give us
heightened sensitivity to the heart of God and to the needs of others. Cooper, C-O-W-P-
E-R, pronounced Cooper, born in 1731, was the fourth child of a British clergyman and
his wife.

William's three siblings died, then his mother died of all giving birth to the fifth
child. William was six when he lost his mother, and it was a blow from which he never
recovered. Years later, when someone sent him a picture of her, he wrote, "'My mother,
when I learned that thou was dead, say, was thou conscious of the tears I shed, hovered
thy spirit or thy sorrowing son? Retch even them,' life's journey just began. I heard
the bell told on thy burial day. I saw the hers that bore thee slow away. And turning
from my nursery window drew a long, long sigh, and wept a last ado.' William emotionally
frail was sent to a boarding school where for two years he was terrorized by a bully
which further shattered his nerves.

From ages ten to eighteen he had a better experience at Westminster School, developing a
love for literature and poetry. His father wanted him to be an attorney, but preparing
for his bar exam he experienced runaway anxiety. Concluding himself damned, he threw
away his Bible and attempted suicide. Friends recommended an asylum run by Dr. Nathaniel
Cotton, a lover of poetry and a committed Christian. His daughter Dr. Cotton's care
William slowly recovered. In the asylum in 1764 he found the Lord while reading Romans
chapter 3, verse 25, whom God set forth as a propitiation by his blood through faith.

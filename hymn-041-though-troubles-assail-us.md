# Though Troubles Assail Us


**Date/source note:** 1779

## Background

1779 In Abraham call the name of the place, the Lord will provide, as it is said to this
day, in the mount of the Lord it shall be provided. Genesis 22. When he quit the
seafaring life, John Newton worked in the docks of Liverpool as a surveyor of tides from
1755 to 1760. During these years he settled down spiritually and began the mature. He
came into contact with the great evangelist George Whitfield and was energized by
thoughts of preaching the evangelical faith. Encouraged by his godly wife Mary, he began
studying Hebrew and Greek and preparing for the ministry.

At age 39 he was ordained, and shortly thereafter appointed to the Anglican Church in
Olney, England. He labored there fifteen years, during which he and the great British
poet William Cooper developed a lasting friendship. The two men met virtually every day
in the garden between their homes, and together they produced a volume of hymns. In 1779
Newton was appointed director of St. Mary's Woolnoth, a quaint church in the heart of
London's financial district. Here he preached for twenty-eight years until his death at
age eighty-two.

He died in life when his mind began failing. He told his friend William J. My memory is
nearly gone, but I remember two things that I am a great sinner and that Christ is a
great Savior. Amazing grace is Newton's best known him, but its popularity has obscured
some of his other compositions. He wrote with incredible insight, yet his words are
simple enough for children. For as Newton's hymn based on Genesis chapter 22 verse 14,

foes all unite, yet one thing secures us whatever batide, the promise assures us, the
Lord will provide.

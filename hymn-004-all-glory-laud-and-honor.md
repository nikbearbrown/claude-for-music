# All Glory, Laud and Honor


**Date/source note:** AD 820

## Background

AD 820. Behold your King is coming to you. He is just in having salvation, lowly and
riding on a donkey, occult the foal of a donkey. XA. 9. XIX The mighty Charlemagne,
742-814, King of the Franks, united most of Western Europe under his crown. He was a
visionary who advanced education and reformed the laws, economy and culture of Europe.
When Charlemagne died, his son, Louis I, assumed the throne. At first all went well, but
in 817 he began dividing the empire among his nephew and his four sons, causing no end
of problems.

Twice he was deposed by his sons, and though he regained the throne both times, he was
never again able to rest securely. Caught in the middle of this epic family conflict,
was Theodolf, Bishop of Orleans, a city south of Paris. Theodolf, born in Spain about
750, had gone to France as a church leader at Charlemagne's request. He was a brilliant
man who worked hard to reform the clergy. He established schools and advanced education.
He advocated high morals, built churches, and composed hymns of praise to God. But
during the political intrigues of Louis's reign, Theodolf was accused, falsely it seems,
of conspiring with King Bernard of Italy, and on Easter Sunday 818 he was imprisoned in
the monastery of Aungie, a city south west of Paris.

There as he meditated on our Lord's triumphal entry into Jerusalem prior to his
crucifixion in resurrection, Theodolf wrote the great Palm Sunday hymn, All Glory, Law,
and Honor. According to a tradition that can be neither confirmed nor denied, when King
Louis later visited Aungie. He momentarily halted by the monastery where Theodolf was
held, and the Bishop appeared at the window singing All Glory, Law, and Honor. The King
was reportedly so moved that he ordered the Bishop's release. For whatever reason we
know Theodolf was released in 821, but he died on his way back to Orleans, or shortly
after his return there.

Originally there were 78 verses, 39 couplets to this hymn. Theodolf had lots of time in
his prison monastery. The first several are the ones we commonly sing today. One stands
that is fallen by the wayside as this quaint verse. Be thou, O Lord, the rider, and we
the little ass, that to God's holy city together we may pass.All glory, Lord and honor

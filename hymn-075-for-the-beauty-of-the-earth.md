# For the Beauty of the Earth


**Date/source note:** 1864

## Background

1864. Therefore you are great O Lord God, for there is none like you, nor is there any
God beside you, according to all that we have heard with our ears. 2 Samuel, chapter 7,
verse 22. Folly at Sandford Peer Point. That's the unlikely name of the author of this
great hymn. Folly at was born October 7, 1835 in Bath, England. After graduating from
Cambridge, he taught at Somersetshire College in his home area of Bath. One day when he
was 29, Folly had found himself walking in the countryside on a beautiful spring day.

He saw the ocean of green, the blue dome of heaven, and the winding Avon River cutting
through the flowery landscape. Overwhelmed with God's creative brilliance, he wrote this
poem. He intended it primarily for communion services in the Anglican Church, but when
it jumped the Atlantic, it quickly became associated with the American Thanksgiving
holiday. In Folly its original version, each verse ended with, Christ our God to thee we
raise, this hour sacrifice of praise. That line was eventually changed to, Lord of all
to thee we raise, this hour him of grateful praise.

Little else is known about Folly at Sandford Peer Point. He resigned from his position
at Somersetshire and apparently moved from place to place, teaching some, writing hymns,
and publishing his poetry. He died in 1917

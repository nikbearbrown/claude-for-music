# The God of Abraham Praise


**Date/source note:** 1404/1770

## Background

1404 and 1770 I am the God of your Father, the God of Abraham, Exodus chapter 3, verse 6
The God of Abraham Praise is perhaps the most Jewish of all Christian hymns and its
writing covers many centuries. Its roots go back to the medieval Jewish scholar Moses
Mamonides, 1135-1204, who wrote a confession of faith containing thirteen creeds.
Centuries later in 1404, another Jewish scholar Daniel Ben Judah, a judge and liturgical
poet in Rome, deeply impressed with Mamonides' creed, composed the Yigdol, a doxology of
thirteen stanzas widely sung in Jewish synagogues to this day.

These later in 1770 an opera vocalist named Meyer Leon sang the Yigdol in London's great
synagogue, Duke's Place. In the audience that night was Thomas Oliver's. Thomas,
1725-1799, had been born in Trejeanen Wales and orphaned about age four. He studied the
craft of shoemaking but he learned the art of sending better, the worst boy known in
Trejeanen for thirty years. When he was eighteen, Thomas was thrown out of town and he
wandered down the Bristol England where evangelist George Whitfield happened to be
preaching from Zechariah chapter three verse two.

Is not this a brand plucked out of the fire? When that sermon began Thomas recalled, I
was one of the most abandoned and proflicate young men living before it ended. I was a
new creature. The world had changed for Tom Oliver's. He became a traveling evangelist
and passionate Christian worker. On that Sabbath evening in 1770 when Thomas heard Meyer
Leon sing the Yigdol, he was so moved that he later approached Leon, acquired the music
and adapted the Jewish doxology into a Christian hymn of thirteen stanzas, beginning the
God of Abraham praise.

Look at this, he told a friend. I have rendered it from the Hebrew giving it as far as I
could a Christian character. Thomas annotated as him with footnotes, citing scripture
references for almost every line, the first being Exodus chapter three verse six. I am
the God of thy father, the God of Abraham. It appeared in 1785 in John Wesley's pocket
hymn book. Modern congregations don't have the patience to sing all thirteen stanzas, so
here is one of the lesser known verses for you to ponder. The God who reigns on high the
great archangel sing, and holy, holy, holy cry Almighty King, who was and is the same
and evermore shall be, Jehovah Lord the great I am, we worship thee.The God of Abraham
Praise

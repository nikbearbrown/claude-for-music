# America the Beautiful


**Date/source note:** 1893

## Background

1893 The heavens are yours, the earth also is yours, the world and all its fullness, you
have founded them. Psalm 89. Verse 11 In 1892 the United States observed the 400th
anniversary of the discovery of America by Christopher Columbus. As part of the
celebration the city of Chicago sponsored a world's fair, which carried over to the next
year. It was in the early summer of 1893 that a group of professors from Wellesley
College visited the exposition on their way to teach summer school in Colorado. The
women later compared the wonders of the man-made affair with the glory of God's
handiwork and the Rockies.

At the close of school the teachers disugd to visit Pike's Peak, elevation 14,000 feet.
One of them, Catherine Lee Bates, later wrote, We hired a prairie wagon near the top we
had to leave the wagon and go the rest of the way on mules. I was very tired, but when I
saw the view I felt great joy, all the wonder of America seemed displayed there, with
the sea-like expanse. It was then and there as I was looking over the sea-like expanse
of fertile country spreading away so far under those ample skies that the opening lines
of the hymn floated into my mind.

When we left Colorado springs the four stanzas were penciled in my notebook. The
Wellesley work soon absorbed time and attention again. The notebook was laid aside, and
I do not remember paying heed to these verses until the second summer following when I
copied them out and sent them to the congregationalist, where they first appeared in
print, July 4, 1895. The hymn attracted an unexpected amount of attention. In 1904 I
rewrote it, tried to make the phraseology more simple and direct. The new version first
appeared in the Boston Evening transcript, November 19, 1904.

There are two stories about the melody, Materna, which was written by Samuel A. Ward,
originally for him entitled, Old Mother Deer Jerusalem. Ward's son-in-law said that the
tune was composed in memory of Ward's oldest daughter, one of the employees at Ward's
music store in Newark, insisted that he compose the tune in 1882, while crossing New
York Harbor after spending the day at Coney Island. The notes came to him so quickly he
jotted them on the cuff of his shirt. Perhaps both stories are true

## Lyrics

O beautiful for spacious skies, for amber waves of grain, for purple mountains'
majesties, above the fruited plain. America, America, God shed His grace on thee, and
crown thy good with brotherhood, from sea to shining sea. America the Beautiful

# JORINDA AND JORINDEL

SONG:

JORINDA AND JORINDEL
(A Poem Inspired by Shel Silverstein and E.E. Cummings)

once in a wood so shadowed and deep,
a castle stood where the lost birds sleep.
a fairy dwelled, with a cruel, sharp gaze,
trapping love in her twilight maze.
the young who wandered, she turned to stone,
or caged as birds in her halls alone.

Jorinda sang by the birch’s sway,
“well-a-day! well-a-day!”
her song broke sharp, her voice undone,
as nightingale notes replaced the sun.
Jorindel froze, like roots in frost,
watching helpless, his love was lost.

the owl flew thrice with a fiery cry,
“tu-whu! tu-whu! the night will lie.”
the fairy came, with her chin and nose,
a hook of darkness where sorrow grows.
Jorinda caged, her song forlorn,
Jorindel wept for the coming morn.

his dreams brought hope, a flower so rare,
purple petals with a pearl to bear.
it whispered soft of the fairy’s doom,
of love restored in the shadowed gloom.
eight days he searched, through hill and glen,
on the ninth, his fate began again.

with the flower’s touch, the castle bent,
doors flew wide where his steps were sent.
birds in cages, their songs all one,
a thousand melodies under no sun.
Jorindel paused, unsure and scared,
which of the voices was hers declared?

the fairy screamed, her rage alight,
but the flower’s glow held back her might.
one cage he touched, and love returned,
as Jorinda’s eyes with fire burned.
arms entwined, they broke the spell,
and freed the birds from their shadowed hell.

love walked free, where the woods once mourned,
the castle empty, its power scorned.
Jorinda and Jorindel made their way,
“well-a-day!” they’d sing, “well-a-day!”
their joy a song, their bond unbroken,
a tale of love where dreams are spoken.

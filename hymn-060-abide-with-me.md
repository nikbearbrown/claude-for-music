# Abide with Me


**Date/source note:** 1847

## Background

1847. If you abide in me and my words abide in you, you will ask what you desire, and it
shall be done for you. John 15. VII. Henry France's light, vicar in the fishing village
of Lower Brickseum, Devonshire, England, ministered faithfully for twenty-three years to
his seafaring people. Though a humble couple, he and his wife Ann lived in an elegant
estate, Berryhead. It had reportedly been provided by King William IV, who had been
impressed with Henry's ministry. At Water's Edge, its coastal views were among the most
beautiful on the British Isles.

Henry laid out walking trails through the estate's forty-one acres and enjoyed the
tranquility of the house and grounds. There he wrote most of his sermons, poems and
hymns. But Henry's lung condition hung over the home like a blackening cloud. Four
Brickseum suffered damp winters, and violent as early fifties Henry realized his lung
disorder had deteriorated into tuberculosis. On September 4, 1847, age 54, he entered
his pulpit with difficulty and preached what was to be his last sermon. He had planned a
therapeutic holiday in Italy.

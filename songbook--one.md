ONE
How Dorothy Saved the Scarecrow

she has (silver) shoes on yellow brick
(through strange lands)
her steps (float)
with a twinkle & a tread
fields of (nowhere) to a place in her head - where
a home waits, somewhere,
& shadows are blue.
she drifts, she hums, she goes,
toto trailing like wings
& she –
starving (wakes) to buttered bread,
to water flowing through this land
& fruit, soft & sweet, golden
in her hands she holds,
a taste for emerald light.

they walk—two wanderers, a path,
past cornfields, a scarecrow.
stuffed with the quiet of straw, he winks
(no mind to lose, but perhaps) he whispers,
“i could learn to think in time.”
she sets him down, they walk,
she in silver, he in blue,
side by side, steps through the fields,
where the sun is a whisper,
& home is a wish, carried lightly like wind,
wrapping them in colors of dusk.

they are strange partners,
she promises to help him,
even if it never ends.
they tread the path, fields stretching wide,
through a world of wishes, dreams,
and silver shoes on yellow brick.

her gingham dress, pink bonnet tied tight,
an old basket, stepping into light.
with silver shoes, replaced, she steps
brave onto streets,
searching for a way home.
they walk, strange companions,
a girl & a straw friend, one step at a time.

& in her heart, a dream
of home.
he says, “I’ll carry the weight, I don’t feel it.
I fear nothing but fire, on this yellow road.”
she laughs, they walk, each with a dream,
toward the City of Emeralds, toward that gleam.
& still, she’s got (silver) shoes on yellow brick,
through fields they roam,
toto at her side, her steps floating,
her heart full of light,
& the wish for a place (called home).

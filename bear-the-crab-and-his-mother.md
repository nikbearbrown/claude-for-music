# The Crab and His Mother

STORY:

"MY child," said a Crab to her son, "why do you walk so awkwardly? If
you wish to make a good appearance, you should go straight forward, and
not to one side as you do so constantly."

"I do wish to make a good appearance, mamma," said the young Crab; "and
if you will show me how, I will try to walk straight forward."

"Why, this is the way, of course," said the mother, as she started off
to the right. "No, this is the way," said she, as she made another
attempt, to the left.

The little Crab smiled. "When you learn to do it yourself, you can
teach me," he said, and went back to his play.

SONG:

The Crab and His Mother
(in the style of Shel Silverstein)

[Verse 1]
Said Mother Crab, with quite a frown,
"Why do you always scuttle 'round?
You zig and zag, you bend and sway-
Why not walk straight like folks today?"

[Verse 2]
The little Crab looked up and blinked,
And gave her words a thoughtful think.
"I'd love to walk in just one line-
Please show me how, and I'll be fine!"

[Verse 3]
"Well this is how," she said with flair,
And skittered sideways through the air.
"No wait," she gasped, "it's more like this,"
As leftward limbs went all amiss.

[Verse 4]
She tried to march both firm and proud,
But veered off sideways through a cloud.
She spun in sand, she danced a jig-
Then tripped upon a kelpy twig.

[Verse 5]
Her son just smiled, polite and bright,
And didn't laugh (though he just might).
"I think," he said, with gentle grace,
"You walk quite sideways every place."

[Verse 6]
"So maybe, Mom, before you preach
You learn the thing you plan to teach.
When you can walk the way you say,
I'll follow right-just name the day!"

[Verse 7]
And off he scuttled, light and free,
Back to the rock beside the sea.
For sometimes kids, though small and spry,
Can see the truth that grown-ups shy.
